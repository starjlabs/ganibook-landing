Best Mint Alternatives in 2026: 6 Budgeting Apps to Consider

By GaniBook · 30 October 2026 · 5 min read

In this article What Should You Look for in a Mint Alternative? 6 Mint Alternatives to Consider Which Mint Alternative Should You Choose? Manual Tracking or Automatic Bank Sync? The Best Budgeting App Is the One You Actually Use

When Intuit shut down Mint in early 2024, many people lost a budgeting app they had relied on for years. For former Mint users, the search for a suitable replacement became an important part of managing their finances.

But finding the right Mint alternative is not simply about choosing another app with charts and spending categories. It is about finding a tool that matches the way you want to manage your money.

Some people prefer automatic bank syncing. Others want to enter transactions themselves, avoid sharing bank credentials and maintain more control over their financial information.

Here are six personal finance apps worth considering, along with the features and trade-offs to think about before choosing one.

What Should You Look for in a Mint Alternative?

Before switching to another budgeting app, consider what you actually used Mint for.

Automatic expense tracking

If you want transactions to appear automatically, look for an app that supports bank connections in your country and with your financial institutions.

Manual expense tracking

If you prefer to record purchases yourself, choose an app with a simple entry process, useful categories and clear reports.

Budgeting tools

Some apps focus on zero-based budgeting, where you assign every dollar a purpose. Others focus on tracking spending and showing where your money goes.

Privacy and control

Check what financial information an app collects, whether it requires bank access and how it handles your data.

Additional money management features

You may also want bill reminders, savings goals, multiple account tracking or a way to record money you lend to friends and family.

6 Mint Alternatives to Consider

1. YNAB — Best for Zero-Based Budgeting

You Need A Budget, commonly known as YNAB, is built around a proactive budgeting method.

Instead of simply showing what you spent, it encourages you to assign every dollar a job. This approach can help people plan ahead, prepare for upcoming expenses and become more intentional with their money.

Best for: People who want a structured budgeting method and are willing to review and manage their budget regularly.

Consider: YNAB requires active participation. It may not suit someone who only wants a simple record of income and expenses.

2. Monarch Money — Best for an All-in-One Financial Overview

Monarch Money offers a broad personal finance experience with budgeting, account aggregation and financial overview tools.

It may appeal to former Mint users who want connected accounts and a central place to review their finances.

Best for: People who prefer an integrated financial dashboard and supported bank connections.

Consider: Review its subscription plans, supported institutions and data-sharing policies before signing up.

3. EveryDollar — Best for a Simple Budgeting Approach

EveryDollar follows a zero-based budgeting approach and is designed to make planned spending easier to understand.

It is an option for people who want to organize their monthly income and expenses around a clear budget.

Best for: People who want a straightforward budgeting process.

Consider: Check which features are included in its free and paid plans and whether automatic transaction importing is available in your region.

5. PocketGuard — Best for Understanding Available Spending Money

PocketGuard focuses on helping users understand how much money may be available to spend after accounting for bills, goals and other financial commitments.

This can be useful for people who tend to overspend or want a quick view of their remaining spending capacity.

Best for: People who want a simple view of their available spending money.

Consider: Check its supported regions, bank connections and plan limitations.

6. Empower — Best for a Broader Financial Picture

Empower offers tools for viewing finances across accounts, including investment and net-worth information.

It may be useful for people whose main priority is seeing a broader picture of their financial position rather than simply recording daily expenses.

Best for: People interested in a combined view of their finances and investments.

Consider: Understand which tools are free, which services are advisory-focused and what information you need to share.

Which Mint Alternative Should You Choose?

The right choice depends on what you want from a personal finance app.

  • Choose YNAB if you want a structured zero-based budgeting method
  • Consider Monarch Money if you prefer connected accounts and a broad financial dashboard
  • Consider EveryDollar if you want a straightforward budgeting approach
  • Choose GaniBook if you prefer manual tracking and want to manage income, expenses, accounts and informal lending in one place
  • Consider PocketGuard if your main concern is understanding available spending money
  • Explore Empower if you want a broader financial overview

Manual Tracking or Automatic Bank Sync?

There is no single method that works for everyone.

Automatic syncing can save time, but it depends on supported institutions, account permissions and reliable connections.

Manual tracking requires a small amount of effort, but it gives you an opportunity to review each transaction as it happens. It can also include cash purchases and other payments that may not appear in a connected bank account.

The important thing is to choose a method you can maintain consistently.

The Best Budgeting App Is the One You Actually Use

A feature-rich app cannot manage your money for you. What matters is whether you use it regularly and understand what your financial records are telling you.

Mint's shutdown reminded people that choosing a financial tool also means considering its long-term usefulness, privacy and reliability.
